### Issue 1: Wrong authors for "Who Is AI Replacing?"
- **Slide**: Class Discussion: "Who Is AI Replacing?"
- **Claim**: "students downloaded and analyzed the paper 'Who Is AI Replacing? The Impact of Generative AI on Online Freelancing Platforms' by Hui, Reshef, and Zhou"
- **Problem**: The paper "Who Is AI Replacing? The Impact of Generative AI on Online Freelancing Platforms" was authored by **Ozge Demirci, Jonas Hannane, and Xinrong Zhu**, published in Management Science (2025). Xiang Hui, Oren Reshef, and Luofeng Zhou are authors of a *different* related paper: "The Short-Term Effects of Generative Artificial Intelligence on Employment: Evidence from an Online Labor Market" (CESifo Working Paper 10601, 2023). These are two distinct studies by different research teams, though both examine AI's impact on freelance labor markets.
- **Suggested fix**: Replace "by Hui, Reshef, and Zhou" with "by Demirci, Hannane, and Zhu" --- or verify which paper was actually used in class. If the Hui, Reshef, and Zhou paper was used, the title needs to be corrected to their paper's title instead.
- **Source**: [Management Science publication](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05420); [SSRN page for Demirci et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4602944); [CESifo page for Hui et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ces/ceswps/_10601.html)
- **Freshness**: Verified March 2026
- **Severity**: **Error** --- incorrect attribution of a published paper

### Issue 2: Nature impact factor is overstated
- **Slide**: Before Reading: Quality Screening (Journal and Impact Factor)
- **Claim**: "Nature (impact factor ~50)"
- **Problem**: Nature's 2024 Journal Impact Factor is **48.5** (per Clarivate JCR 2024, published 2025). The text says "~50" which is an approximation, but it rounds up rather than down. The 5-year IF is 55.0. The IF peaked at 69.5 in 2022 due to COVID citation inflation and has since normalized. The "~50" figure was closer to accurate for the 2021 IF (49.962) but is slightly high for the current figure.
- **Suggested fix**: Change to "impact factor ~49" or "impact factor around 48-50" if keeping it approximate. If precision matters for the teaching point, use "48.5 (2024)."
- **Source**: [Nature Journal Metrics](https://www.nature.com/nature/journal-impact); [ManuSights analysis](https://manusights.com/blog/nature-impact-factor)
- **Freshness**: Verified March 2026
- **Severity**: **Imprecision** --- the approximate figure is close but rounds in the wrong direction

### Issue 6: "roughly 10% of jobs in OECD countries disappear every year, and about 10% of entirely new jobs emerge"
- **Slide**: Class Discussion: "Who Is AI Replacing?"
- **Claim**: "roughly 10% of jobs in OECD countries disappear every year, and about 10% of entirely new jobs emerge"
- **Problem**: Job destruction and creation rates of approximately 10% per year have been documented in specific studies (e.g., UK manufacturing: ~10% creation, ~13.5% destruction). However, these rates vary significantly by country, sector, and time period. The OECD has published research on creative destruction and labor market dynamics, but a single "10% and 10%" figure as an OECD-wide average is a simplification. The symmetry of "10% in, 10% out" is particularly neat and may be a pedagogical approximation rather than a precise statistic.
- **Suggested fix**: Consider adding "approximately" or "on the order of" to signal this is a rough figure, or cite a specific source. The number is in the right ballpark for many developed economies but presenting it as an OECD-wide fact without a source may overstate precision.
- **Source**: OECD "Coping with Creative Destruction" report; Barnes & Haskel (SSRN) on UK manufacturing job flows
- **Freshness**: Verified March 2026
- **Severity**: **Imprecision** --- the order of magnitude is correct but the symmetric "10% and 10%" framing may be too tidy
